The global supply chain disruptions that began in 2021 revealed critical vulnerabilities in just-in-time manufacturing. Container shipping costs, which averaged around 1,500 dollars per container before 2020, peaked at over 10,000 dollars in 2021. These disruptions affected industries from semiconductor manufacturing to automotive production, costing the global economy an estimated 800 billion dollars in lost output. Taiwan produces approximately 65 percent of the world's semiconductor chips and over 90 percent of advanced-node chips used in smartphones and computers. Recognizing this concentration risk, major economies including the United States and the European Union have launched initiatives to develop domestic semiconductor manufacturing capacity. The U.S. CHIPS Act provided 39 billion dollars in subsidies and funding for chip production facilities. Meanwhile, reshoring efforts are reshaping international trade patterns, with companies reconsidering their reliance on single-country suppliers. Economic models suggest that diversifying supply chains could reduce disruption risks by 20 to 30 percent, though at an estimated global cost of 200 billion dollars in increased production expenses. Trade economists debate whether this localization trend will ultimately strengthen economic resilience or reduce efficiency gains from global integration.
