Autonomous vehicles (AVs) are moving from experimental prototypes toward commercial deployment, but cost remains a major barrier to widespread adoption. A single self-driving sensor suite, combining LiDAR, radar, and high-resolution cameras, can add $10,000 to $100,000 to a vehicle's price, depending on the level of autonomy targeted. Companies like Waymo and Tesla have taken different approaches: Waymo relies on expensive LiDAR-heavy systems for high reliability in limited areas, while Tesla bets on cheaper camera-based systems paired with machine learning to scale faster across broader markets. Industry analysts estimate that LiDAR costs have fallen by over 80% since 2015, and further price drops are expected as production scales. Beyond hardware, AV developers face steep costs in training and validating AI models, often requiring millions of simulated and real-world driving miles. Looking ahead, researchers are focusing on improving edge-case handling, such as unpredictable pedestrian behavior and adverse weather, as well as developing standardized safety validation frameworks. Some experts predict that fully autonomous vehicles could become cost-competitive with human-driven cars by the early 2030s, though regulatory approval and public trust remain significant hurdles alongside the technical and financial ones.
