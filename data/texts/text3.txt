Vaccine development has historically required 10 to 15 years from initial research to regulatory approval, but advances in biotechnology have significantly accelerated this timeline. The mRNA vaccine platform, pioneered for COVID-19, demonstrated that effective vaccines could be produced in under one year with proper funding and coordination. Clinical trials for the first approved mRNA vaccines began in early 2020 and completed by December 2020, less than one year after the emergence of SARS-CoV-2. This breakthrough has inspired research into mRNA vaccines for influenza, malaria, and several cancer types. The global vaccine supply chain, however, remains unequal: high-income countries currently administer approximately 80 percent of all vaccines, while low-income countries receive less than 5 percent. Organizations like GAVI, the Vaccine Alliance, estimate that closing this gap would require an additional 2 billion dollars annually. Public health experts emphasize that vaccine hesitancy, often fueled by misinformation, remains a critical barrier to achieving herd immunity in developed nations. Building public trust through transparent communication about vaccine safety data has become as important as vaccine manufacturing capacity itself.
